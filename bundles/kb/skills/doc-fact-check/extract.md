# fact-check — stage E brief (extract)
You are a fact-check stage-E worker. Read only this brief, your batch file and `brain_context.json`. No Brain calls, no subagents, no other steps. Follow the command rules: absolute paths, the Write tool for output, no inline Python.

### 2. Extract atomic claims
Walk paragraphs in order. Keep only checkable statements. Types: NUM (numbers, %, counts), TIME (dates, timelines, current/legacy/planned), ENTITY (system, vendor, version names), TOPO (what connects to what, hosts, environments), OWN (who owns/operates), STATUS (live / retired / in migration). Skip opinions, intentions, headings, boilerplate.
Each claim: `id` (C01…; in stage E the claim id field is `claim_id` (`B<k>-C<n>`), renumbered to `C01…` by the merge), `p_id`, verbatim quote ≤25 words, type (figure transcriptions (`I*`) are exempt from the 25-word limit). Every claim carries `section`: its `section` is the heading path of its paragraph (e.g. `1 Scope > 1.2 Systems`), "Figure" for `I*`. An empty `section` is invalid. For a claim spanning several sentences, use the `s_id` of the sentence holding its main assertion as `s_id` (the anchor) and list every sentence the claim spans, the anchor included, in `s_ids`; the claim then lists every sentence it spans in `s_ids`, so none of them is reported as unclaimed. Merge duplicates. Extract **every checkable statement**: there is no count target and no sampling. Every paragraph, list item and table row that holds a checkable statement yields at least one claim, and every type (NUM, TIME, ENTITY, TOPO, OWN, STATUS) is covered wherever it occurs; do not stop once the NUM and TIME claims are in.

**Gap claims are checkable and must be extracted.** Sentences that say something is undecided, not established, unclear, missing, or out of scope are STATUS or OWN claims — the Brain may have a source that contradicts the gap:
- `The speaker did not establish whether system A pushes the result into system B or B reads it.` → OWN (who controls that path?)
- `"Future responsibility remains undecided as legacy functions move."` → OWN/STATUS (is there a documented owner or decision?)
- `"does not isolate the integration package boundary"` → TOPO (does the Brain document the boundary?)
- `"the session did not cover the authentication model"` → STATUS (does the Brain have an auth source?)
Extract every such sentence as a claim of type OWN, STATUS, or TOPO. Do not skip them as uncheckable.

**Always extract these, whatever else you skip:**
- absolute or universal statements (never, always, all, none, no, only, every, consistent across);
- ownership, attendance and responsibility statements (who owns, operates, attended, decided);
- every number with a unit or count, and every date;
- every quotation attributed to a person, as its own claim (its wording is checked against the source), even when the same sentence also holds a number or a date (in a Fast run the quotation is that sentence's one claim, see below);
- every table header row or chart label that says what its figures measure ("attempt rates by intent", "calls per session"): the label is a claim, because a correct figure under the wrong label is a borrowed statistic relabelled.

**Coverage self-check (end of step 2).** List every heading of the document (for a batch: every section of the batch). Each heading must appear as the `section` of at least one of your claims, or in your coverage record, `{section_id, reason}` with reason "no checkable statement" (only for a section with no claims at all; a single tagged sentence with nothing checkable gets `{s_id, reason}` instead). A batch writes that record to `coverage_batch_<k>.json`; the merge combines the records later. `merge_findings.py` enforces section coverage: it fails on any section of a batch with neither a claim nor a coverage entry (`chunk_claims.py` applies the same check, so a stage-E gap is caught before stage V). Re-walk any heading that has neither before verifying. For orientation only, never as a cap: a typical 10–15 page baseline draft yields 50–90 claims.

## Fast (scope risk): one claim per sentence
In a Fast run, make at most one claim per in-scope sentence or row, and one for each such sentence or row you do not waive: its main assertion, with s_ids for a claim that spans sentences. A sentence is in scope when its batch-file risk list is non-empty; sentences with an empty risk list are not extracted in a Fast run. When a quotation attributed to a person and a figure (a number or a date) share a sentence, the claim is the quotation, and its `questions` cover the figure as well as the wording. chunk_claims.py refuses a second in-scope claim anchored on the same sentence. In a Deep run, extract every checkable statement as before, untagged sentences included (the quotation and the figure are then two claims).

## Claim: the statement made self-contained
`quote` is the verbatim span (the anchor); `claim` is what stage V checks. Write `claim` as one sentence of at most 40 words (a deck claim: 55) that a reader could check without seeing the draft: name the subject, the period or as-of date, the scope and the unit, resolving pronouns and fragments, using **only** the quote, the sentence or row it sits in, its section (`section`, `heading_path`), the block's `ctx` (`parent`: the bullet or list item it belongs to; `header` and `row_label`: what a table cell measures; `footnote`: a source or as-of line on the same slide; `attribution`: where the statement was said or written, a meeting or document with a time or date) and the batch's `doc_date`. Example: quote `99.9%` in a row with `header: ["Metric", "Value"]`, `row_label: "Uptime"`, under the heading "North region — FY2025" → claim "Uptime in the North region was 99.9% in FY2025." Copy every number and date exactly as the draft writes it ("FY25" stays "FY25"). Never add a fact, name, number or date from outside the draft; `chunk_claims.py` refuses a `claim` holding a number that its quote and context do not contain. When the context does not supply something the claim needs to be checked, do not guess: list it in `unresolved` (any of `subject`, `period`, `scope`, `unit`), e.g. a bare "99.9%" under an undated heading → `unresolved: ["period"]`. A complete sentence that already stands alone keeps its wording as the claim. When the section carries `slide_context` (a deck slide's title, lede and key), name the slide's scenario in a few words ("In the phone-change flow, …"); stage V also receives the whole slide context. Copy `section` exactly as the batch file writes it. For a deck claim (its section has `slide_context`), the quote is the whole sentence that holds the risk-tagged words; a sentence over 25 words is cut to the clause that holds them (the same statement then gets the same quote in every run). When the block carries `attribution`, the claim says where the statement was made ("In <source> at <time>, a speaker said that …"); the attribution is part of that claim, never a claim of its own.

## Questions
Every claim carries "questions": 1–3 plain-language questions, written from its `claim` (never from a bare fragment), naming the facts the claim rests on, the way someone would ask the Brain, not the draft's sentence: "each job runs on four schedules, one per database" → ["how many databases are there", "how often does each job run"]; "the archive purges data after 60 days" → ["how long does the archive keep data"]. Write the subject in plain words; never paste the claim as the question.

## Output
Write `<work dir>/claims_batch_<k>.json`: a top-level list of `{claim_id: "B<k>-C<n>", p_id, s_id, section, quote, claim, type, questions}` plus an optional `s_ids` (every `s_id` the claim spans, the anchor included) and an optional `unresolved`. `s_id` is the anchor, copied from the batch file's sentence or row, and `section` is copied from its section. In `<work dir>/coverage_batch_<k>.json`, write `{s_id, reason: "no checkable statement"}` only for a tagged sentence with nothing checkable (a heading-like row, a restatement already covered by another claim); a section-level entry `{"section_id": …, "reason": "no checkable statement"}` is only for a section with no claims at all, and the tools reject one for a section that has a claim. A Fast (`risk`) stage E claims or waives every risk-tagged sentence and row of its batch (a claim covers a sentence as its `s_id` anchor or by listing it in `s_ids`); a Deep (`all`) stage E needs no waivers for tagged sentences. A batch with no checkable claims still writes `claims_batch_<k>.json` as `[]`.

**Before you finish, check your files.** Run `python "<this brief's folder>/chunk_claims.py" "<work dir>" --scope <your scope> --check-batch <k>` (the work dir is your batch file's folder; the scope is `all` or `risk` as your dispatch says). It lists only your batch's problems and writes nothing. Fix each one in your own `claims_batch_<k>.json` / `coverage_batch_<k>.json` and run it again; finish only when it prints `batch <k>: OK`.
